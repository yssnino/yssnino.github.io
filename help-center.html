Flight delayed or cancelled?
You've got this. Let's sort it out.

Disruptions are stressful, especially with kids in tow. Here's a calm, step-by-step plan, tools to find a backup flight, and your rights explained simply, with links to the official rules.

Your first 15 minutes

1. Get in two lines at once

Join the line at the gate or help desk and contact the airline by app, chat, or phone at the same time. Whoever answers first can usually rebook you.

2. Check the airline app

Many airlines offer self-service rebooking in their app. Look before the best seats on the next flights are gone.

3. Know what you want

Decide your plan A and plan B: a later flight today, a flight tomorrow, a nearby airport, or a refund if you'd rather not travel.

4. Ask, kindly and clearly

"Can you rebook us on the next available flight, including partner airlines?" If it's an overnight cancellation the airline caused, ask about a hotel and meal vouchers.

5. Keep every receipt

Screenshot the delay notice and keep receipts for meals, hotels, and transport. You may need them for a claim.

6. Take care of the crew

Yours, that is. Water, snacks, charge phones, find a quiet corner. Our Kids' Zone has free games and calm-down breathing.

Find a backup flight

Search other airlines' flights for today or tomorrow. If you hold a ticket, talk to your airline before buying a new one, because you may be rebooked for free or be owed a refund.

Your rights on US flights, in plain language

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules can change, so the official source links are included for each point.

Refunds when the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight

For flights to, from, or within the US, if the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight and you choose not to travel or accept rebooking or a voucher, you're entitled to a refund of the ticket, including taxes and fees, to your original form of payment. "Significant change" includes:

  • A domestic flight departing 3+ hours earlier or arriving 3+ hours later than scheduled
  • An international flight departing 6+ hours earlier or arriving 6+ hours later
  • A different departure or arrival airport, more connections, or a downgrade to a lower class

Refunds are due within 7 business days for credit card purchases and 20 calendar days for other payment methods. If you accept a voucher instead, that's your choice. The airline must tell you about your refund right first.

Sources: US DOT: automatic refund rule · 14 CFR Part 260

Delayed bags and services you paid for

If you file a mishandled-bag report and your checked bag isn't delivered within 12 hours of a domestic flight's arrival (15 or 30 hours for international, depending on flight length), you're entitled to a refund of the checked bag fee. You can also get a refund for extras you paid for but didn't get, like Wi-Fi or seat selection.

Meals and hotels: check the airline's commitments

US law doesn't require airlines to pay cash compensation for delays, but major US airlines have publicly committed to things like meal vouchers when an airline-caused delay or cancellation means a wait of 3+ hours, and hotel rooms for overnight airline-caused cancellations. The DOT dashboard shows each airline's commitments.

Source: US DOT Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard

Stuck on the plane?

For US airlines, a tarmac delay generally can't exceed 3 hours on domestic flights or 4 hours on international flights before passengers are given the chance to get off, with exceptions for safety, security, and air traffic control. Food and water must be offered within 2 hours of a tarmac delay starting.

Source: US DOT: Fly Rights · 14 CFR 259.4

Sitting together as a family

Some airlines guarantee fee-free adjacent seating for young children traveling with an adult. Check the DOT's family seating dashboard and book seats early.

Source: US DOT Airline Family Seating Dashboard

Need to complain?

Start with the airline. If that doesn't resolve it, you can file a complaint with the US Department of Transportation.

File an air travel complaint with DOT

EU & UK flights (EU261 / UK261)

Who's covered: flights departing from an EU airport (any airline), and flights arriving in the EU on an EU airline. Similar rules (UK261) cover flights departing the UK, and flights arriving in the UK on a UK or EU airline.

Compensation: if you arrive at your final destination 3 or more hours late, your flight is cancelled with less than 14 days' notice, or you're denied boarding, you may be owed fixed compensation, unless the cause was "extraordinary circumstances" such as severe weather or air traffic control restrictions.

Care while you wait: depending on flight distance and delay length, the airline must offer meals and refreshments, a way to communicate, and a hotel plus transport if you're delayed overnight, even when the cause was extraordinary.

Refund or rerouting: if your flight is cancelled, you can choose a refund or rerouting. For long delays (5+ hours), you can choose a refund if you no longer want to travel.

Coming change: the EU adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/2202, which updates these rules. It applies from 23 October 2027. Until then, the current rules above apply.

Sources: Your Europe: air passenger rights · Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 · UK CAA: your rights

Canada: Canada has its own Air Passenger Protection Regulations. See the Canadian Transportation Agency.
